# House rules for this repo's video projects (videos/)

- **Fix script mistakes immediately.** When the user's script contains a factual error, correct it directly in the
  script you hand back (voiceover lines, captions, aligner text) and briefly say what changed and why. Don't just flag it.
- **Voice speed is always 1.2x.** Every ElevenLabs recording is sped up 1.2x (pitch-preserving) before syncing.
  `tools/align_vo.py` does this by default.
- Write ElevenLabs lines with tone tags (`[deadpan]`, `[dramatic]`…), `…` pauses, CAPS for emphasis and spelled-out numbers.
- The user often works from a phone: always paste the voiceover lines directly in chat, not only in a file.
- When pasting a script for ElevenLabs, do NOT number the lines: plain paragraphs only, so it copies and pastes cleanly.
- **Characters:** when a country speaks or acts, show a person who represents it (an officer, diplomat, leader type…)
  wearing that country's flag as a cape, instead of a plain flag-with-eyes. Flag markers are still fine on maps.
- **No generic pins for places with meaning:** when a map marker stands for something (an empire, a colonial capital,
  a fort, a spaceport, a port, an oil field…), draw what it represents instead of a pin: e.g. a castle flying the ruling
  country's flag (and flip the flag when control changes), a launch pad, a lighthouse, an oil rig. Round flag pins are only
  for simply naming a country.
- Style blueprint: Map Warden-like 9:16 map explainers (teal ocean #1E4D5C, olive land #8A855C, flag characters
  with stick arms and eyes, white 2–4 word captions, gold title cards, yellow kinetic numbers).
- **Hands stay visible:** when a character holds a prop (shovel, key, flag, scroll, tablet…), draw the hand on top of the prop, and never let an arm or hand hide behind the body or head.
- **Script structure (proven by the Japan islands and Canberra Shorts, use it by default):** (1) a fact everyone believes, stated plainly; (2) the surprise, straight away; (3) rule out the obvious explanation fast; (4) ONE cause told as a mini story (who, when, what happened); (5) ONE everyday analogy; (6) a concrete payoff number; (7) end on a short punchline, or, for a Short that leads to a long-form, a CONCRETE twist (a specific surprising fact, never a vague tease like "a fear nobody's heard of"). No stock hooks ("you've had this wrong your whole life"), no stacking several reasons, no forced "take a guess" questions in the voice. Put the comment question on screen + in the pinned comment instead. A loop ending is fine only when the last line flows naturally into the first.
- **Teaser Shorts must stand alone:** a Short that leads to a long-form answers its own hook with a real payoff (never "find out in the full video"), and its twist opens a NEW question that only the long-form answers. Aim for about 55–65 s. The long-form opens with a quick recap of the Short and then tells the next part as a story.
- **Shorts always end with a comment question on screen** (on the end/cliffhanger card, e.g. "WHICH COUNTRY WOULD YOU PICK? 👇"), matching the pinned comment. Never in the voice.
- **Check every project at least 3 times before sending:** (1) stills of every scene: overlaps, clipped/shrunk text, hands visible, labels on screen, captions readable; (2) timing: BEATS/END_HOLD match the voice (last beat includes END_HOLD), card shows, duration right, voice cuts in silences, loop frames match; (3) final mp4: duration, loudness ≈ −14 LUFS, frames pulled from the actual video at key moments (incl. first/last frame and each version, e.g. YouTube + TikTok). Fix anything found and re-check before sending.
- **Titles: always offer 5–8 options**, each with a one-line reason (curiosity gap, number, contradiction, search term…), and mark a recommended pick. Keep each under ~60 characters.
- **Descriptions: the right length, not the shortest.** The goal is breakout content: the first line is a hook that works on its own (it shows in search/feed), then 1–2 short paragraphs that tell the story with natural search keywords, the comment question, and 3 hashtags (+ chapters for long-forms). Cut padding and repetition, never the substance. Rules here are means to that goal: apply them with judgment, not literally.
- **Audience priority (RPM):** frame content for US, Canada, Australia and UK viewers first (miles first, comparisons to places they know, post ~17:00–20:00 UTC). Long-forms especially should target these countries and their search terms (place names like "australia", "canada", "sydney"). Shorts can stay global: their views and subs count toward monetization from anywhere.
- **Shorts length follows the story (≈50–75 s):** the top Shorts ran 58–76 s at ~85–90% viewed. Keep the end card short (~1.5 s) and put the question on the last spoken line: retention drops in the final seconds.
- **Long-forms only for searchable topics whose Short performed** (>~2,500 views or ~70% stayed). Titles are curious questions that don't give away the answer (Tibet 4.84% CTR vs Canberra 0.96%).
- **Visual polish:** big numbers tick up on a rolling counter (miles first, km after); tease key reveals with a black silhouette for ~1 s; give cutaways their own backdrop (ice / blueprint / parchment) instead of the dimmed map; keep something moving every 2–3 s; every pop-in gets a sound. Borrow techniques, never another channel's format, topic angle or signature gags. Helpers: `odometer()`, `silhouette()`, `cutWorld()` in `videos/lakes-short/src/app.js`.
- **Long-form pacing (from the hemisphere video's retention graph):** show the core map answer as a visual 'aha' within the first 25–30 s, then use the history and examples as proof. Never list dates or examples for a full minute before the reveal (retention fell from 87% to 48% between 0:21 and 1:06 there). Broad, whole-globe questions get the best click rates from Home and Up Next (4–6% CTR), so favour universal map mysteries for long-forms.
- **Long-forms are numbered (Australia-middle video: 69.2% viewed):** promise the structure in the first 20 s ("it comes down to three things", "six chokepoints", "#6 to #1"), then signpost every part ("First…", "Number four…"), with each part opening on its own mini-hook. Use a countdown (#N→#1, best saved for last) when the topic is naturally a list, and "N reasons/clues" when it's a single question. The map answer still comes in the first 30 s.
