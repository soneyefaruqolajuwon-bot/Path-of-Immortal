Psy

The tiny caretaker who lives inside your head and runs your brain. Every Short, Psy works a different room: the editing suite, the memory library, the spotlight rig, the projector booth.

Role
Your brain's overworked staff of one. Finds things out with the viewer, never lectures.
Personality
Curious, a little clumsy, warm, quietly mischievous.
Signature
Ends every episode by tapping the bulb on his cap (it lights up), then pointing at the viewer.
Easter egg
A small red pin with a gold star on his cap, for the dream of studying in China. Long-time subscribers will spot it.
Plate 1 · Construction

How Psy is built

  1. One shape. The body is a single soft circle, slightly taller than wide (height ≈ 1.05 × width). No neck. Head and body are the same thing.
  2. Eye line sits just below the middle. Eyes take up about a third of the body width. Big eyes carry all the acting.
  3. The cap covers the top third and is always worn the same way: brim turned to Psy's left (screen right).
  4. Bulb clip on the front-left of the cap. Off by default; on only for ideas and the sign-off.
  5. Short limbs. Arms reach no higher than the cap brim unless he stretches. Three fingers and a thumb.
  6. Ink outline is thick and even: about 2% of character height. No sketchy lines.
  7. Silhouette test: filled solid black, Psy must still read as Psy (round body + cap + brim). Check every new pose this way.
Plate 2 · Colour

Palette

Psy's colours never change between episodes, whatever room he's in. Backgrounds can shift; Psy can't.

Plate 3 · Acting

Expressions and poses

The six states used in episode 01. Each one is a separate artwork layer so it can be swapped on a cut.

Plate 4 · Rules

Keeping Psy consistent

Always

  • Show Psy within the first 2 seconds of every Short.
  • Keep his cap, colours and proportions identical to this sheet.
  • Let him react to the science: shock, effort, a smug grin. The reaction is the joke.
  • End with the bulb tap and point.
  • Keep him in the lower two thirds of the frame, clear of the top title area and the right-side buttons.
  • Add all on-screen words in the edit, never inside the artwork.

Never

  • Give him a lab coat, glasses or a pointer stick. He's a worker, not a professor.
  • Let him talk straight to camera for more than one line. He shows, the captions tell.
  • Draw a nose. Psy has no nose (useful for episode 01).
  • Change the brim direction or drop the cap.
  • Use realistic or 3D renders of him. Flat vector only.
  • Crowd him into every shot. Some shots are just the idea.
Episode 01

Your Brain Is Hiding Your Nose

Seven shots, about 26 seconds. Psy works in the editing room, where the brain cuts your nose out of your view all day. Use the base style prompt at the start of every image prompt so every shot matches.

Base style (start of every prompt)

Flat 2D vector illustration, bold clean shapes, thick even dark-navy outlines (#1E1B3A), soft two-tone cel shading, subtle paper grain texture, warm cinematic lighting with cyan accent glow, limited palette of navy, cyan, peach and warm yellow, vertical 9:16 composition, no text, no letters, no logos.

Psy reference sheet (make this first, then reuse it)

Character turnaround of a small cute mascot named Psy: a single round soft body slightly taller than wide, peach-pink skin (#F6B3A3) with darker peach shading, very large expressive eyes with navy pupils and white highlights, small simple mouth, rosy cheeks, no nose, short stubby arms with three fingers and a thumb, short stubby legs, wearing a cyan baseball cap (#29C7C0) with the brim turned to the side, a small yellow lightbulb clipped to the front of the cap, a tiny red pin with a gold star on the cap. Front view, three-quarter view, side view and back view, plus six facial expressions. Plain white background, flat vector, thick navy outline.

Artwork specs for the edit

Backgrounds1080 × 1920 px minimum (2160 × 3840 preferred), PNG. No Psy, no text in them.
PsyEach pose on its own transparent PNG, at least 1600 px tall. Arms on a separate layer where possible, so they can move.
PropsEraser, rubber stamp, signal parcels, levers, signs and spotlight: each on its own transparent PNG.
DepthSplit each room into back, middle and front layers. That's what gives the camera moves real depth.
TextNone in the art. Labels, captions and sign wording are added in the edit, so they stay sharp and can be changed.
