# Bolivia — ElevenLabs voiceover (corrected)

[curious] Most countries on Earth are named after ancient tribes, rivers, or geographic features. [matter-of-fact] But Bolivia is one of the few named after a real person… the very man who freed it.

[conversational] In the early nineteenth century, much of South America was ruled by the Spanish Empire — until a Venezuelan military leader named Simón Bolívar led a massive wave of independence wars across the continent.

[intrigued] He helped liberate territory that eventually became six modern countries — and when a region called Upper Peru finally broke free from Spain, local leaders wanted to honor the man who made it possible.

[amused] At first, they officially named their brand-new country the Republic of Bolívar. [wry] Which sounded a bit too much like a personal fan club — so just two months later, a congressman suggested tweaking the ending so it sounded more like a real nation.

[confident] That small spelling change gave the world Bolivia — a permanent reminder on every map of the one general who reshaped South America.

## Corrections made
- "one of the only nations in the world named directly after a single real person" → many countries are named after people
  (Colombia/Columbus, the Philippines/Philip II, Saudi Arabia/House of Saud…). Rewritten to "one of the few named after a real
  person… the very man who freed it".
- "a few months later" → "just two months later" (República Bolívar, August 1825 → Bolivia, 3 October 1825).
