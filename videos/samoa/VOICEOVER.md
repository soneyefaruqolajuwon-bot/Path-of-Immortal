[curious] Imagine going to sleep on a Thursday night… and waking up the next morning to find out it's suddenly SATURDAY. [matter-of-fact] That isn't a sci-fi movie — it actually happened to the ENTIRE island nation of Samoa.

[conversational] For more than a century, Samoa sat on the eastern side of the International Date Line, keeping its calendar synced with American traders in California. [intrigued] Back then, that setup made total sense for shipping and business.

[wry] Over time, however, Samoa's biggest trading partners shifted to Australia and New Zealand — which sit right next door across the Pacific… yet were nearly a FULL DAY ahead on the calendar.

[confident] So in December twenty-eleven, Samoa's government decided to jump across the Date Line. [dramatic] At midnight on Thursday… the line on the map was bent around their eastern shore — completely DELETING Friday, December thirtieth, and jumping straight into SATURDAY.

[playful] Even wilder, neighboring American Samoa decided NOT to switch… meaning today, you can hop on a thirty-minute flight between the two islands and literally travel TWENTY-FOUR HOURS backward… or forward in time.

## What changed from the draft script

| 5 | "decided to physically redraw the map… they bent the International Date Line" → "decided to jump across the Date Line… the line on the map was bent around their eastern shore" | The Date Line isn't a legal border anyone owns. Facts confirmed: east of the line since 1892 (US/California trade), skipped Fri 30 Dec 2011 (Thu 29 → Sat 31),
two shared workdays a week lost, American Samoa kept its time, ~30-minute flight Apia ↔ Pago Pago, 24-hour difference.
