# Singapore — ElevenLabs voiceover (corrected)

[curious] Almost every country on Earth had to fight a war, launch a revolution, or sign a hard-fought treaty to win its independence. [matter-of-fact] Singapore is the only modern nation in history that got kicked out of its own country against its will.

[conversational] In the early nineteen-sixties, after nearly a hundred and fifty years of British rule, Singapore merged with neighboring territories to form the brand-new Federation of Malaysia. [intrigued] On paper, the union made perfect sense: Singapore had one of the busiest ports in Asia, while Malaysia had the land and natural resources to back it up.

[serious] Behind closed doors, however, deep political and racial tensions quickly boiled over. Leaders in Kuala Lumpur clashed bitterly with Singapore's government over economic control, political representation, and who should hold ultimate power.

[tense] Riots broke out in the streets, trust completely collapsed, and instead of risking a full-scale civil war, Malaysia's parliament made an unprecedented decision. [matter-of-fact] In a unanimous vote — with Singapore's own representatives absent — they passed a law expelling Singapore from the federation altogether.

[somber] Just hours later, Singapore's prime minister, Lee Kuan Yew, announced the split in a televised press conference — breaking down in tears in front of the entire nation. [concerned] Overnight, a tiny island with almost no fresh water, no natural resources, and barely an army was left completely on its own.

[confident] Many doubted Singapore could survive. [enthusiastic] Instead, forced to survive by sheer strategy and trade, that unwanted island transformed itself into one of the richest, most powerful financial hubs on the planet.

## Corrections made
- "after centuries of British colonial rule" → "after nearly a hundred and fifty years of British rule" (British from 1819; merger 1963).
- "went on live television" → "announced the split in a televised press conference" (9 August 1965).
- "no army" → "barely an army" (Singapore had two small infantry battalions in 1965).
- "Most experts assumed Singapore would collapse within a few years" → "Many doubted Singapore could survive" (documented sentiment, no overclaim).
